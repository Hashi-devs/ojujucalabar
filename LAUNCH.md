## Google Play store listing

**App name** (30 characters max)
Ojuju Calabar

**Short description** (80 characters max)
Escape your father’s haunted house before Ojuju Calabar finds you.

**Full description**

Every Nigerian child heard the warning: be quiet, or Ojuju Calabar will come.

October 1997. Tobi Adeleke, 26, is sent on a job to Oke-Ọla, the hill town he left at four, the night his mother ran down the hill in the rain. At the top of the hill stands his father’s house. Nobody climbs that hill. Twenty-two years to the night, Tobi does, and the doors slam behind him.

Something tall walks that house, in raffia, with a carved face. The children call it Ojuju Calabar. It knows Tobi’s name. It wants its own back.

Ojuju Calabar is a first-person horror escape game, made in Nigeria and set in a real Nigerian home.

• Seven haunted rooms: the Living Room, Tobi’s Room, Mama's Kitchen, the Boys’ Quarters, Papa's Office, Mama’s Prayer Room and the Master Bedroom. Then a run across the zinc roof to the chimney nobody ever lit.
• Comic-strip cut scenes, and oil paintings that change as you learn what happened in 1975.
• Puzzles in every room. Find clues, crack locks and combine what you learn.
• Fourteen hidden memory pages tell what happened to the Adeleke family and their driver, Effiong. Find them all for the true ending.
• Ojuju Calabar hunts you. Hide when he comes, and when something calls your name, do not answer.
• Jump scares that hurt. Your lamp goes out, your hands shake and your fear builds.
• Bush lamps, Ankara, a box TV under lace, highlife on the record player, Pidgin whispers in the dark: horror that feels like home.
• Seven cowrie shells hidden in the house for collectors.
• Saves automatically, even in the middle of a room.
• Gentle mode for players who want the story without the jump scares.
• Low graphics mode for budget phones.
• A live speedrun leaderboard for Haunted mode. No accounts needed.

Play with headphones. Turn the lights off.

A Muniac Studios game.

## 30-second trailer script

Record from the game (screen recording on your phone in landscape), then cut it together in CapCut or InShot. Use the game's own sound. No music needed until the very end.

| Time | Shot | Text on screen / sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Black. Then the loading screen: MUNIAC STUDIOS presents | Sound: a single heartbeat |
| 3–6 s | Walking into the Living Room: the fan turning, the bush lamp, the box TV | Text: *Oke-Ọla. 1997.* |
| 6–9 s | Tap the TV: the face in the static | Text: *Every clock stopped at 7:20.* |
| 9–12 s | Mama's Kitchen: lighting the cooker, pounding the mortar | Text: *Find what the house is hiding.* |
| 12–15 s | The brass drawer dials rolling, the padlock shackle jumping open | Sound: the click and the unlock |
| 15–18 s | The Boys’ Quarters: Ojuju unfolding out of the long crate | Text: *Hide.* Sound: footsteps |
| 18–21 s | Inside the water drum, the view through the gap, his legs passing | Sound: breathing, sniffing |
| 21–24 s | "Something is calling your name. Don't move." | Text: *Do not answer.* |
| 24–27 s | The roof chase toward the chimney, quick cuts | Sound: running, stomps getting closer |
| 27–29 s | Jump scare: Ojuju's face, full screen | Sound: the shriek. Cut to black on the scream. |
| 29–30 s | Title: OJUJU CALABAR. "Coming soon on Android" | The highlife guitar from the title screen |

**Tips**
- Keep each shot to 2–3 seconds. Horror trailers work by showing less.
- End on the jump scare, then the title. People rewatch the scare.
- Post a vertical version too: crop the middle for TikTok, Reels and WhatsApp status.
- First caption idea: "Nigerian parents used Ojuju Calabar to scare us. We made him a game."
